Lost in Translation: Connectedness, Willingness to Communicate and Display of Agency
Abstract publication reference

This study examines how foreign students in a Philippine university navigate their sense of connectedness, their willingness to communicate in the classroom, and their ability to express agency. Drawing on both surveys and interviews, the findings reveal that while students generally feel socially connected with their peers, this does not consistently translate into active classroom participation.

A weak yet positive relationship was identified between students’ sense of connectedness and their willingness to communicate, suggesting that feeling included is important but not sufficient to fully encourage engagement. Various linguistic, cultural, and confidence-related factors continue to influence students’ readiness to speak up and assert themselves in academic settings.

The study underscores the importance of fostering inclusive, supportive, and culturally responsive learning environments. By creating spaces that value diverse voices and encourage active participation, institutions can better empower students to communicate confidently and exercise their agency—contributing to a more dynamic, collaborative, and globally relevant academic community.

Classroom Management of PATHFit Courses in the Onsite Learning: A Phenomenological Study
Abstract publication reference

This study delves into the lived experiences of instructors teaching PATHFit (Physical Activity Towards Health and Fitness) courses in the return to onsite learning. Using a phenomenological approach, it captures how educators navigate the dynamic demands of managing student behavior, sustaining engagement, and facilitating physical activities in face-to-face settings.

The findings highlight a range of adaptive strategies employed by instructors to maintain discipline, motivate participation, and ensure student safety—particularly in physically active environments. At the same time, the study surfaces key challenges, including varying student readiness, health considerations, and the need to re-establish routines disrupted during remote learning.

Overall, the research underscores the importance of responsive and student-centered classroom management approaches that promote well-being, active participation, and a supportive learning atmosphere. These insights contribute to strengthening instructional practices that align with holistic development and resilient, health-oriented educational environments.

The university’s research publication data over the past four years presents a dynamic picture of expanding participation alongside evolving productivity outcomes. In 2022, 7 out of 31 faculty researchers successfully published their work, achieving a strong 22.6% publication rate and reflecting a solid foundation of scholarly engagement within a relatively small research community.

In 2023, the number of researchers increased significantly to 139; however, no publications were recorded during this period. This shift highlights a crucial disconnect between research involvement and the successful dissemination of outputs, underscoring the need for strengthened institutional support systems, clearer publication pathways, and sustained mentorship.

By 2024, faculty participation further expanded to 232 researchers, with 6 publications produced, equivalent to 2.6%. While this indicates a gradual recovery in research output, it also emphasizes the importance of enhancing research capacity, strengthening collaboration, and providing targeted interventions to improve publication outcomes.

In 2025, research productivity showed notable progress, with 14 publications generated from the same pool of 232 researchers, representing 6.03%. This more than twofold increase compared to the previous year signals a positive shift toward improved research efficiency, stronger support mechanisms, and a growing culture of scholarly dissemination within the institution.

Overall, the trend reflects a university in transition—one that is broadening its research base while steadily improving its capacity to produce publishable outputs. Moving forward, strengthening research ecosystems, fostering interdisciplinary and sustainability-driven initiatives, and enhancing incentives for publication will be essential in translating increased participation into high-quality, impactful, and globally competitive research contributions.

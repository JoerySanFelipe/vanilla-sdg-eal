Urdaneta City University (UCU) continues to strengthen its commitment to sustainability and responsible waste management through a comprehensive assessment of organic waste generation, aligned with the UI GreenMetric World University Rankings, particularly under the Waste (WS) and Setting & Infrastructure (SI) indicators. The assessment provides data-driven insights into the composition and volume of biodegradable waste on campus, supporting evidence-based planning and continuous environmental performance improvement.

The findings indicate a total of 11.3 tons of organic waste, composed entirely of leaf waste. This highlights the significant contribution of campus green spaces, landscaped areas, and tree maintenance activities to overall organic waste generation. Seasonal variations, such as leaf shedding, further influence the volume of biodegradable materials collected, emphasizing the importance of adaptive and sustainable landscape management practices.

Notably, the assessment recorded no measurable food waste, reflecting the effectiveness of UCU’s food service management and waste minimization strategies. This outcome suggests the successful implementation of practices such as portion control, efficient meal planning, responsible consumption, and awareness campaigns within the campus community. These efforts demonstrate alignment with sustainability principles and contribute to minimizing avoidable waste streams.

Given the substantial volume of leaf waste, UCU has a strong opportunity to further enhance its sustainability initiatives through the expansion of composting systems. By converting organic waste into nutrient-rich compost, the University can:

  • • Reduce dependence on landfills and lower greenhouse gas emissions;
  • • Support campus landscaping, urban gardening, and greening programs;
  • • Close the loop in resource use by promoting a circular approach to waste management.
